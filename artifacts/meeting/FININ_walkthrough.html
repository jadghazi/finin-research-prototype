FININ, at student scale

A working prototype of the paper's news interaction model, tested on SPY and public financial headlines. This page is self-contained and opens offline on an 8 GB laptop.

1 · The question

Can a model that lets headlines interact, then weighs them against the market, predict the next trading session's direction better than simpler methods on the same dates?

2,107usable dated examples
16maximum headlines per example
317held-out test dates
18trained runs across 3 seeds

The original paper used millions of Reuters news items. This study uses NIFTY headlines, SPY prices, and finance-trained TinyBERT sentiment as a documented smaller experiment.

3 · What we tested

Every method uses the same 317 test dates. Six trainable variants run with seeds 17, 42 and 73. Model checkpoints were chosen using validation loss.

Result to say aloud: The full model predicted up on 312–317 of 317 test dates across its three seeds. Only 184 dates actually rose. Balanced accuracy near 50% means this run gives no convincing evidence that headline attention improved direction predictions. This is a useful measured outcome of the prototype.

Balanced accuracy averages success on up and down days. Log loss also assesses the probability estimates. ± is variation across training seeds, not uncertainty across future years.

Probability reference added during the audit: A constant probability fitted only on training labels (0.5471) has test log loss 0.6824, compared with 0.6816 for reduced FININ. This is a fairer probability comparison than the always-up probability of 1.

5 · What this does and does not show

Reproduced from the paper

Separate text and numerical encoders, news self-attention, market-query news weighting, a weighted news summary, and a final direction predictor.

Changed for this study

SPY replaces the index, NIFTY replaces Reuters, BGE-small and a compact financial sentiment model supply frozen features, headline count is capped, news is delayed one trading day, and one chronological split replaces ten windows.

The measured results answer this prototype's question on this historical period. They do not numerically reproduce the Reuters study or establish a tradable strategy.
